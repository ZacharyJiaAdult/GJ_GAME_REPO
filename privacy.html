Privacy

Quiltopia is a world everyone builds together. We collect as little as we can to run it: no chat, no free typing, no photos, no location, and no ads.

Last updated 13 October 2026

The short version

  • You can play as a guest. We don't ask for your name, email or age.
  • Your builder name comes from word lists, so you can't type anything personal into the game.
  • Other players see your builder name, nation (if you pick one), level, looks, home and what you build.
  • We don't sell your information, and we don't show ads or use ad trackers.
  • You can delete your account any time in Settings → Delete my account.

What we collect and why

A guest ID
When you first play, our sign-in provider makes a random ID for this device so your home and progress are kept. It doesn't contain your name or email.
Your game
Your builder name (from word lists), the nation you choose (optional), everything you build, and your progress: level, Buttons, Stitchbook, outfits, friends, gifts, stickers, the Blocks you've visited, and who invited you.
Google sign-in (optional)
If you choose to sign in with Google so your progress follows you to other devices, we receive your email address and Google account ID. We use them only to sign you in. Players under 13 need a parent or guardian to do this for them.
Reports
If you report a patch, we keep which patch it was, the reason you picked from the list, and who sent it, so a moderator can check it.
How the game is used
Simple game events tied to your player ID, like opening the game, sending an invite, or filling a Wonder slot. We use them to see whether the game works and is fun, never for advertising.
The Quiltopia Club (optional, paid)
Payments are handled by Stripe on Stripe's own page. We never see your card. We keep your Club end date and Stripe's subscription ID. Players under 18 need a parent or guardian to buy it.
On your device
The game stores your settings (sound, night lighting, national features), unsent actions, and a few play counts in your browser's local storage. Clear your browser data to remove them.
Technical data
Like every website, the services that run Quiltopia see your IP address and basic request details when your browser connects to them.

Who helps us run it

  • Supabase: our database and sign-in. It stores your account and your game.
  • Netlify: hosts the website.
  • Stripe: takes Club payments.
  • Google: Google Fonts serves the game's lettering, and Google handles Google sign-in if you use it.
  • jsDelivr: serves the code library that connects the game to Supabase.

They use your information only to provide their service to us.

What other players can see

Quiltopia is shared, so your builder name, nation, level, looks, home, what you build, your name on Wonder plaques, your Club badge, and stickers and gifts you send are visible to other players. Your bag, Buttons, friends list and reports are private.

Children

Quiltopia is made to be safe for all ages. There's no chat and no free typing, every name is built from word lists, and nothing anyone builds can be removed by another player. Children can play as guests without giving us any personal details. Google sign-in needs a parent or guardian for players under 13, and the Club needs one for anyone under 18.

Parents and guardians can ask us to show or delete their child's information at the address below, or delete it straight away in Settings → Delete my account.

Keeping and deleting

We keep your information while your account exists. Settings → Delete my account removes your account, your sign-in, your progress, your own builds and your game events straight away. Patches you gave to a Wonder stay, so other players' Wonders aren't broken, but your name comes off them.

You can also write to us to get a copy of your information, correct it, or delete it. We look after your information under New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020. If you're unhappy with how we've handled it, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner.

Changes

If we change this page, we'll update the date at the top. If a change matters, we'll tell players in the game.
